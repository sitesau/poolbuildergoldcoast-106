Four local option lenses, not four brand names

Without publishing fixed prices or build periods, the honest comparison is a set of lenses. Use them to test what each conversation is actually offering.

  • Design-led pathway: ask how shell geometry, coping, water edge and adjoining paving or landscaping will be recorded before price talk hardens.
  • Constrained-access pathway: ask what excavation route is assumed, what structures constrain plant, and which inclusions change if access narrows.
  • Level-and-drainage pathway: ask which grade, retaining, cut, fill and runoff questions remain unresolved, and who investigates them.
  • Compliance-and-integration pathway: ask whether pool fencing, barrier access and nearby outdoor works are treated as design inputs rather than later accessories.

The strongest option is rarely the one with the smoothest first answer. It is the one that makes uncertainty visible, then prices it or excludes it clearly.

What belongs in a written scope before proposals are compared

A comparable written scope should separate the items that verbal talk tends to blur. Inclusions state what is supplied. Allowances state provisional amounts or selections that may move. Exclusions state what is outside the contractor responsibility. Approval assumptions state who is expected to obtain what, and what happens if rock, poor access, existing retaining or drainage conditions alter the plan.

This matters because a short figure can still be useful when it says what is firm, what is provisional and what awaits site investigation. It is weak when broad phrases such as standard finish, adequate drainage or council approval assumed carry no line item behind them. Those phrases can shift risk back to the owner once excavation begins.
